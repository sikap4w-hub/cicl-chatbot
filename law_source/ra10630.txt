REPUBLIC ACT NO. 10630 — AN ACT STRENGTHENING THE JUVENILE JUSTICE SYSTEM IN THE PHILIPPINES, AMENDING FOR THE PURPOSE REPUBLIC ACT NO. 9344, OTHERWISE KNOWN AS THE "JUVENILE JUSTICE AND WELFARE ACT OF 2006" AND APPROPRIATING FUNDS THEREFOR
Approved: October 3, 2013
(This law amends specific sections of RA 9344. Each section below states which RA 9344 section it amends and replaces.)

Section 1. The Title of Republic Act No. 9344 is hereby amended to read as follows: "An Act Establishing a Comprehensive Juvenile Justice and Welfare System, Creating the Juvenile Justice and Welfare Council under the Department of Social Welfare and Development, Appropriating Funds Therefor, and for Other Purposes."

Section 2. (amends RA 9344 SEC. 4, Definition of Terms — adds): "Bahay Pag-asa" refers to a 24-hour child-caring institution established, funded and managed by local government units (LGUs) and licensed and/or accredited nongovernment organizations (NGOs) providing short-term residential care for children in conflict with the law who are above fifteen (15) but below eighteen (18) years of age who are awaiting court disposition of their cases or transfer to other agencies or jurisdiction. Part of the features of a Bahay Pag-asa is an intensive juvenile intervention and support center (per Sections 20, 20-A and 20-B). A multi-disciplinary team composed of a social worker, a psychologist/mental health professional, a medical doctor, an educational/guidance counselor and a Barangay Council for the Protection of Children (BCPC) member shall operate the Bahay Pag-asa, working on the individualized intervention plan with the child and the child's family.

Section 3. (amends RA 9344 SEC. 6, Minimum Age of Criminal Responsibility — this is the current, controlling version): A child fifteen (15) years of age or under at the time of the commission of the offense shall be exempt from criminal liability. However, the child shall be subjected to an intervention program pursuant to Section 20 of this Act. A child is deemed to be fifteen (15) years of age on the day of the fifteenth anniversary of his/her birthdate. A child above fifteen (15) years but below eighteen (18) years of age shall likewise be exempt from criminal liability and be subjected to an intervention program, unless he/she has acted with discernment, in which case, such child shall be subjected to the appropriate proceedings in accordance with this Act. The exemption from criminal liability herein established does not include exemption from civil liability, which shall be enforced in accordance with existing laws.

Section 4. (amends RA 9344 SEC. 8, Juvenile Justice and Welfare Council — current, controlling version): The JJWC is created and attached to the Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD), placed under its administrative supervision (previously it was under the Department of Justice). Chaired by an Undersecretary of DSWD. Coordinates: DOJ, Council for the Welfare of Children (CWC), DepEd, DILG, Public Attorney's Office (PAO), Bureau of Corrections (BUCOR), Parole and Probation Administration (PPA), NBI, PNP, Bureau of Jail Management and Penology (BJMP), Commission on Human Rights (CHR), TESDA, National Youth Commission (NYC), and other juvenile justice institutions. Composed of representatives (rank not lower than director) from DOJ, DSWD, CWC, DepEd, DILG, CHR, NYC, two NGO representatives, DOH, and one representative each from the Leagues of Provinces, Cities, Municipalities and Barangays. A Regional Juvenile Justice and Welfare Committee (RJJWC) exists in each region, chaired by the DSWD regional director, ensuring implementation at the regional/LGU level, composed of similar member-agency representatives at the regional level.

Section 5. (amends RA 9344 SEC. 9, Duties and Functions of the JJWC — current version; also adds SEC. 9-A, Duties and Functions of the RJJWC): JJWC duties: oversee implementation of the Act; advise the President on juvenile justice policy; assist agencies in policy formulation; develop a 3-to-5-year national juvenile intervention program; coordinate intervention programs nationally; consult local government leagues; formulate policies with children's participation for delinquency prevention and CICL rehabilitation; collect data and conduct research on juvenile justice trends and needs; submit an annual report to Congress; ensure children's involvement in research/policy; conduct regular and spot inspections of detention/rehabilitation facilities; coordinate personnel training; submit an annual report to the President. RJJWC duties (SEC. 9-A) mirror these at the regional/LGU level: oversee regional implementation, assist in policy compliance, develop the regional 3-to-5-year juvenile intervention program, coordinate regional programs, oversee the region's Intensive Juvenile Intervention and Support Center, collect regional data, conduct regional facility inspections, coordinate regional trainings, and report annually to the JJWC.

Section 6. (amends RA 9344 SEC. 20, and adds SEC. 20-A, 20-B, 20-C, 20-D, 20-E — these are the current, controlling provisions for how children below/around the age of criminal responsibility are handled):

SEC. 20. Children Below the Age of Criminal Responsibility. — If the child taken into custody is fifteen (15) years old or below, the authority with initial contact, in consultation with the local social welfare and development officer (LSWDO), must immediately release the child to the custody of parents/guardian, or in their absence, the nearest relative. The child undergoes a community-based intervention program supervised by the LSWDO, unless the child's best interest requires referral to a youth care facility or Bahay Pag-asa managed by LGUs or licensed/accredited NGOs monitored by DSWD. The LSWDO determines appropriate programs in consultation with the child and custodian. If parents/guardians/nearest relatives cannot be located or refuse custody, the child may be released to: (a) a duly registered NGO or religious organization; (b) a barangay official or BCPC member; (c) the LSWDO or DSWD. If found dependent, abandoned, neglected or abused, and placement in a youth care facility/Bahay Pag-asa is in the child's best interest, the parents/guardians execute a written authorization for voluntary commitment; if unable/refusing, DSWD/LSWDO files a petition for involuntary commitment under PD 603. The minimum age for commitment to a youth care facility/Bahay Pag-asa is twelve (12) years old.

SEC. 20-A. Serious Crimes Committed by Children Who Are Exempt From Criminal Responsibility. — A child above twelve (12) up to fifteen (15) years of age who commits parricide, murder, infanticide, kidnapping and serious illegal detention where the victim is killed or raped, robbery with homicide or rape, destructive arson, rape, carnapping where the driver/occupant is killed or raped, or drug offenses under RA 9165 punishable by more than twelve (12) years imprisonment, is deemed a neglected child under PD 603 and mandatorily placed in the Intensive Juvenile Intervention and Support Center (IJISC) within a Bahay Pag-asa. The LSWDO (or DSWD social worker in their absence) files a petition for involuntary commitment within twenty-four (24) hours of the report. The court decides on the petition within seventy-two (72) hours of filing, and determines an initial placement period of at least one (1) year, based on reports from the IJISC's multi-disciplinary team.

SEC. 20-B. Repetition of Offenses. — A child above twelve (12) up to fifteen (15) who commits an offense a second time or more, having previously undergone a community-based intervention program, is deemed a neglected child under PD 603 and undergoes an intensive intervention program supervised by the LSWDO; if the child's best interest requires Bahay Pag-asa placement, the same voluntary/involuntary commitment process under SEC. 20 applies.

SEC. 20-C. Exploitation of Children for Commission of Crimes. — Any person who uses, takes advantage of, or profits from a child in committing a crime, including abuse of authority or confidence to induce/threaten/instigate the crime, receives the maximum penalty prescribed by law for that crime.

SEC. 20-D. Joint Parental Responsibility. — The court may require parents of a CICL to undergo counseling or other intervention, based on recommendation of the IJISC multi-disciplinary team, LSWDO, or DSWD. "Parents" means biological parents, adoptive parents, or individuals with custody of the child. Parents are liable for damages unless they prove reasonable supervision and diligence to prevent the offense.

SEC. 20-E. Assistance to Victims of Offenses Committed by Children. — The victim and victim's family are provided appropriate assistance and psychological intervention by the LSWDO, DSWD, and other concerned agencies.

Section 7. (amends RA 9344 SEC. 22, Duties During Initial Investigation — current, controlling version): The law enforcement officer determines where the CICL's case should be referred. The child's statement is taken in the presence of: (1) the child's counsel of choice, or a PAO lawyer in their absence; (2) the child's parents, guardian, or nearest relative; and (3) the LSWDO. In the absence of the parents/guardian/relative and LSWDO, the investigation proceeds in the presence of an NGO representative, religious group, or BCPC member. The social worker conducts an initial assessment — using DSWD-developed discernment assessment tools — to determine appropriate interventions and whether the child acted with discernment (without prejudice to a later, more comprehensive case study report). The local social worker then: (a) proceeds under Section 20 if the child is 15 or below, or is 15–17 and acted WITHOUT discernment; or (b) if the child is 15–17 and acted WITH discernment, proceeds to diversion.

Section 8. (amends RA 9344 SEC. 33, Preliminary Investigation and Filing of Information — current version): The prosecutor conducts a preliminary investigation when: (a) the child doesn't qualify for diversion; (b) the child/parents/guardian don't agree to diversion; or (c) the prosecutor, considering the social worker's assessment, determines diversion isn't appropriate. Upon serving the subpoena and complaint affidavit, the prosecutor notifies PAO of the service and the child's personal information/place of detention. Upon finding probable cause, the information is filed before the Family Court within forty-five (45) days of the start of preliminary investigation, and must allege the child acted with discernment.

Section 9. (amends RA 9344 SEC. 49, retitles Youth Detention Homes to "Bahay Pag-asa" — current, controlling version): Each province and highly-urbanized city (LGU) is responsible for building, funding, and operating a Bahay Pag-asa within its jurisdiction, per DSWD standards adopted by the JJWC. Every Bahay Pag-asa has an Intensive Juvenile Intervention and Support Center (IJISC) for children under Sections 20, 20-A, 20-B, requiring a more intensive multi-disciplinary intervention program, with standards set by the JJWC in partnership with DSWD, DOH, DepEd, and DILG.

Section 10. (amends RA 9344 SEC. 50, Care and Maintenance of the Child in Conflict with the Law — adds): LGUs' expected expenditures on the local juvenile intervention program for children at risk and CICL shall be included in the LGU's annual budget. Highly-urbanized cities and provincial governments must include a separate budget for constructing/maintaining the Bahay Pag-asa, including the IJISC's operation within it.

Section 11. (amends RA 9344 SEC. 57, Status Offenses — current version; adds SEC. 57-A): SEC. 57. Status Offenses. — Any conduct not considered an offense, or not penalized, if committed by an adult shall not be considered an offense and shall not be punished if committed by a child. SEC. 57-A. Violations of Local Ordinances. — Local ordinances on juvenile status offenses (curfew violations, truancy, parental disobedience, anti-smoking and anti-drinking laws) and light offenses/misdemeanors against public order or safety (disorderly conduct, public scandal, harassment, drunkenness, public intoxication, criminal nuisance, vandalism, gambling, mendicancy, littering, public urination, trespassing) are for the protection of children. No penalty is imposed on children for these violations — instead they are brought to their residence or to a barangay official at the barangay hall, released to the custody of parents. Appropriate intervention programs (counseling, group activities for children, parenting seminars for parents) must be provided in such ordinances. The child is recorded as a "child at risk," NOT as a "child in conflict with the law."

Section 12. (new — Mandatory Registry of Children in Conflict with the Law): All duty-bearers (barangay/BCPC workers, law enforcers, teachers, guidance counselors, social workers, prosecutors) who receive, handle, or refer CICL cases must faithfully record all pertinent information (age, residence, gender, crime, and details of the intervention/diversion undergone) to guarantee correct application of the law. The JJWC leads establishment of a centralized information management system on CICL.

Section 13. (amends RA 9344 SEC. 63, Appropriations — current version): Funding is charged against the JJWC's current-year appropriations under the DOJ budget; thereafter included in DSWD's annual budget under the General Appropriations Act. Four hundred million pesos (P400,000,000.00) is appropriated for constructing Bahay Pag-asa rehabilitation centers in high-CICL-incidence provinces/cities, identified by DSWD and JJWC, coursed through DPWH. LGUs provide a counterpart share of five million pesos (P5,000,000.00) per rehabilitation center.

Section 14. Implementing Rules and Regulations. — The JJWC promulgates the necessary IRR within sixty (60) days of this Act's effectivity. (This is the Revised IRR document — see irr_9344_10630.txt.)

Section 15. Separability Clause. — If any provision is held unconstitutional, other unaffected provisions remain valid and binding.

Section 16. Repealing Clause. — All inconsistent laws, decrees, ordinances and rules are modified or repealed accordingly.

Section 17. Effectivity Clause. — This Act took effect fifteen (15) days after publication in the Official Gazette or in at least two (2) national newspapers of general circulation (approved October 3, 2013).
